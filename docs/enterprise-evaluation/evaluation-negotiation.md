# Prepare a commercial proposal and negotiate responsibly

[Track home](README.md) · [Scenario and templates](toolkit.md)

**Format:** Available reading lesson and manual practice. **Basis:** Original teaching synthesis using the named sources below. **Review:** Draft for editorial review; no professional certification.

## Understand

Negotiation starts with interests and alternatives. Prepare the economics, scope and authority behind an offer before discussing concessions.

BATNA is your best alternative if no agreement is reached; a reservation point is the least acceptable agreement given your alternatives and constraints.

Ask what drives a discount request. Budget predictability, scope and payment timing may matter more than headline price.

Prepare approved trades and equivalent packages. Do not promise unapproved roadmap, legal or service commitments.

Use genuine deadlines and objective evidence. Never invent a competitor, alternative offer or customer commitment.

## See it in the fictional scenario

A narrower scope may address a budget limit while preserving unit economics. Compare what each package delivers and excludes rather than hiding a reduction in service.

## Try

Prepare a standard-scope option and a narrower-scope option with synthetic prices. List interests, BATNA, approval owner, tradeables and walk-away conditions without publishing an internal reservation price in the customer proposal.

## Explain your decision

The customer requests a discount. What should happen first?

1. Concede immediately
2. Understand the constraint and prepare an authorized trade
3. Invent a deadline

**Answer:** 2. Price is one term within an agreement. Understand the underlying interest and your authority before proposing changes.

## Review your artifact

- Does it identify the decision and responsible owner?
- Can another person distinguish facts, assumptions and unknowns?
- Does each conclusion point to evidence or an explicit missing item?
- Have you documented an alternative and a condition that would change your recommendation?

Reading is **reviewed**; producing the artifact is **practiced**. A correct knowledge answer passes only that check. These are distinct from demonstrated workplace competence.
