# Build a value hypothesis and executive brief

[Track home](README.md) · [Scenario and templates](toolkit.md)

**Format:** Available reading lesson and manual practice. **Basis:** Original teaching synthesis using the named sources below. **Review:** Draft for editorial review; no professional certification.

## Understand

Translate a technical capability into a customer outcome and an observable test. Keep the business case honest about uncertainty.

Connect the current problem to a required capability, an expected outcome and evidence that could support it.

A useful insight invites the buyer to reconsider an assumption using credible evidence; it does not manufacture fear.

Separate measured time savings, estimated capacity and cash savings. Avoid adding overlapping benefits.

An executive brief needs a decision request, alternatives, constraints and open risks, not a feature inventory.

## See it in the fictional scenario

Use the executive brief to ask for approval of a bounded evaluation. A claim about preventing breaches would require evidence this exercise cannot provide.

## Try

Calculate an illustrative capacity estimate using 100 reviews per month, 12 baseline minutes and 7 trial minutes per comparable review. Explain why saved time is not automatically reduced payroll or avoided breach cost.

## Explain your decision

The sample suggests faster triage. Which claim is defensible?

1. We guarantee fewer breaches
2. The sample suggests capacity savings, subject to representativeness and adoption
3. The annual subscription pays for itself regardless of price

**Answer:** 2. Five minutes times 100 reviews is 500 minutes, about 8.3 hours per month. It is a scenario estimate, not a measured customer outcome.

## Review your artifact

- Does it identify the decision and responsible owner?
- Can another person distinguish facts, assumptions and unknowns?
- Does each conclusion point to evidence or an explicit missing item?
- Have you documented an alternative and a condition that would change your recommendation?

Reading is **reviewed**; producing the artifact is **practiced**. A correct knowledge answer passes only that check. These are distinct from demonstrated workplace competence.
