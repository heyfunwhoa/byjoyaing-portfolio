# Choose a sales framework for the job

[Track home](README.md) · [Scenario and templates](toolkit.md)

**Format:** Available reading lesson and manual practice. **Basis:** Original teaching synthesis using the named sources below. **Review:** Draft for editorial review; no professional certification.

## Understand

A methodology helps you think; a process describes stages; a checklist records actions. Use a framework to answer a specific question rather than forcing every acronym into every call.

SPICED organizes customer context: Situation, Pain, Impact, Critical Event, Decision. SPIN groups questions around Situation, Problem, Implication and Need-payoff.

MEDDIC covers Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Identify Pain and Champion. MEDDICC adds Competition; MEDDPICC adds Paper Process. MEDDICC’s current teaching uses Implicate the Pain.

Sandler helps establish mutual expectations. Challenger emphasizes teaching, tailoring and guiding decisions. Command of the Message connects customer outcomes and differentiated value.

BANT covers Budget, Authority, Need and Timeline; treat missing budget as a question about funding, not an automatic rejection.

These are practitioner frameworks. This combined teaching sequence is an original synthesis, not a certified course or proof of improved win rates.

## See it in the fictional scenario

Use SPICED to understand the problem, MEDDPICC to inspect decision evidence, and a shared plan to coordinate actions. A budget owner and an authorized signer may be different people.

## Try

For each unknown in the scenario, choose one framework and explain what evidence you would seek. Do not write seven separate discovery scripts.

## Explain your decision

An enthusiastic evaluator says the demo looks great. What should you do next?

1. Mark the champion and budget confirmed
2. Ask how the decision will be made and seek evidence of influence and funding
3. Require every acronym to be completed during this call

**Answer:** 2. Enthusiasm is evidence of interest, not authority, influence or funding. Use the smallest helpful framework.

## Review your artifact

- Does it identify the decision and responsible owner?
- Can another person distinguish facts, assumptions and unknowns?
- Does each conclusion point to evidence or an explicit missing item?
- Have you documented an alternative and a condition that would change your recommendation?

Reading is **reviewed**; producing the artifact is **practiced**. A correct knowledge answer passes only that check. These are distinct from demonstrated workplace competence.
