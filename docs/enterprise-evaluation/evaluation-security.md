## Understand

A security review assesses risk for a defined use. Its scope depends on access, data and criticality; an evaluation and production deployment may require different reviews.

Start with architecture and data-flow scope, then gather the requested evidence with owners, dates and applicability.

Distinguish a questionnaire answer from supporting evidence and an approved risk decision. Unknown answers go to the accountable expert.

Review access, encryption, logging, vulnerability management, incident response, continuity, data lifecycle and relevant subprocessors.

Share restricted reports through approved channels. A SOC report or certificate must match scope and period; it is not blanket approval.

Privacy applicability depends on actual processing and jurisdiction. The ICO reference concerns UK GDPR processor contracts; it is not a rule that applies identically everywhere.

## See it in the fictional scenario

An approval for synthetic evaluation data does not automatically cover production records. Reassess the changed scope before production use.

## Try

Complete the review tracker for an unanswered retention question and a requested penetration-test summary. Assign owners, sharing restrictions and evidence dates; leave approval pending.

## Explain your decision

A questionnaire asks about a control you cannot verify. What should you do?

1. Answer yes to avoid delay
2. Mark it unknown and route it to the control owner with an evidence request
3. Treat a roadmap item as implemented

**Answer:** 2. Accurate scope and evidence matter more than a complete-looking questionnaire. Only the designated risk owner can accept an exception.

## Review your artifact

- Does it identify the decision and responsible owner?
- Can another person distinguish facts, assumptions and unknowns?
- Does each conclusion point to evidence or an explicit missing item?
- Have you documented an alternative and a condition that would change your recommendation?

Reading is **reviewed**; producing the artifact is **practiced**. A correct knowledge answer passes only that check. These are distinct from demonstrated workplace competence.
