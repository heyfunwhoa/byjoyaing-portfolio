# Evidence-based product requests — Builder Academy

**Purpose:** Help a salesperson, CSM, support specialist, marketer, or business leader submit a request with enough information for discovery. A request is not a roadmap promise.

## Request quality checklist
1. Who has the problem and what are they trying to achieve?
2. What is their current workflow or workaround, and what is failing?
3. What original evidence, date, and speaker or research source supports the claim? Use an approved company system for real confidential details.
4. How frequently does it happen and to how many affected users? Distinguish one anecdote from a validated pattern.
5. What measurable outcome matters (hours saved, errors prevented, retention risk reduced)?
6. What is the customer value, commercial value and strategic fit? What are the delivery costs, security risks and alternatives?
7. What is still unknown, and what evidence might disconfirm the assumption?
8. Who decides and how will the submitter get an answer?

**Weak fictional request:** “Build PDF export. It's blocking a $200k deal.”

**Stronger fictional request:** “A fictional AppSec buyer reported spending four hours per month reconciling three audit reports. Current workaround: manual spreadsheets. Source: dated fictional discovery note, uncorroborated by other customers. Desired outcome: under 30 minutes with traceable evidence. PDF export is one possible solution; other approaches should be explored. One $200k opportunity has exposure but the incremental likelihood of winning attributable to this change is unknown.”

## Quantifying responsibly
- Customer value: time, error reduction, reduced risk or faster completion. Validate users, frequency and fully loaded costs.
- Commercial impact: distinguish total pipeline exposure from incremental, *attributable*, probability-adjusted opportunity value. Example: $200k opportunity × 15 percentage point hypothetical win-probability lift = **$30k illustrative expected bookings**, not $200k guaranteed ARR.
- Strategic fit: ICP reach, differentiation, compliance/security and platform reuse. Qualitative value may matter even where attributable ARR is unclear.
- Count an opportunity once, never add exposure and incremental value together, and do not equate bookings, revenue and cash.
- Consider confidence ranges, counterfactual outcomes, opportunity costs and implementation cost before treating an estimate as an ROI.

## Intake decisions
**Needs more evidence**, **Discovery candidate**, **Workaround**, **Defer/not now**, or **Decline** are all valid outcomes. Accepted proposals still need research, priority, feasibility and a canonical PRD before engineering tasks are created.

## How this connects
Feedback originates from company-controlled sources. Product evaluates requests. Linear is the committed engineering backlog, not an automatic mirror of all raw requests. GitHub stores the owning product's requirements, code and release evidence. Technical Docs stores generic reusable quality standards. Builder Academy teaches the process with synthetic exercises. Keep Joy Index and company records separate.

## Evidence reference and provenance (fictional training example)

Record **source system**, approved source reference/ID, observation date, recording timestamp or note section, relevant speaker if allowed, claim classification (direct quote vs AI summary vs interpretation vs hypothesis), evidence confidence, access permission, and the exact product-problem claim supported. Keep the full recording/transcript in the original authorized system; references are not permission grants. Corrections and withdrawn evidence should be traceable. A Gong call and a Granola summary of the *same* account are corroborating artifacts, **not** two independent customers. Real CRM ARR or pipeline data should only be linked through an employer-approved RevOps model, with clear distinctions between exposed and incremental attributable value.
